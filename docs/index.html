Nothing to Declare? · A disclosure check of medical journals, 2023–2026

One in three medical papers has an author who didn't declare drug-company money.

Papers end with a promise: "The authors declare no conflicts of interest." Nobody checks it. We compared those statements with the US government's public record of payments from drug and device makers to doctors. Half the papers pass cleanly. A third don't.

So what

What a journal can do on Monday.

Check every submission

Run the declaration against the public payment record automatically, before review. It takes seconds per paper.

Ask, don't accuse

Send the author the evidence with a polite query. Most gaps are omissions, fixed with one updated line.

Publish with confidence

Readers, reviewers and regulators trust disclosures that have been checked. That is a quality mark competitors don't have.

How we checked

Method and limits.

1 · PapersOpen-access papers in 40 clinical journals with US authors, Oct 2023 – Oct 2026. Declarations read from the full text.
2 · MatchingEach US author matched to one doctor in CMS Open Payments by name, state and city. Ambiguous matches are left out.
3 · PaymentsFees, grants, royalties, travel and meals that companies reported in the 3 calendar years before publication.
4 · VerdictRed when a paying company or its corporate group is missing from the declaration. An AI model reads partial declarations.

A red flag is a question for an editor, not a finding of misconduct. Journal policies differ on what must be declared, and some disclosures reach the editor without being printed. Only US clinicians appear in the payment record. Authors are anonymised to specialty and US region; amounts are rounded to $1,000, and amounts over $100,000 appear only as bands. A hand check of 20 random red cases found about 9 in 10 correct.

Data: Europe PMC (papers), CMS Open Payments 2020–2025 (payments).
