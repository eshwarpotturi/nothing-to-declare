Nothing to Declare? · A disclosure check of medical journals, 2023–2026

Nearly 3 in 10 papers hide drug-company money.

Medical papers ask authors to declare money from drug and device companies. We checked 2,878 papers against the US government's public payment record. More than half pass. Nearly 3 in 10 do not.

So what

What a journal can do on Monday.

Check every submission

Run the declaration against the public payment record automatically, before review. It takes seconds per paper.

Ask, don't accuse

Send the author the evidence with a polite query. Most gaps are omissions, fixed with one updated line.

Publish with confidence

Readers, reviewers and regulators trust disclosures that have been checked. That is a quality mark few journals can show.

    How we checked

    Method and limits.

    1 · PapersOpen-access papers in 40 clinical journals with US authors, Oct 2023 – Oct 2026. Declarations read from the full text.
    2 · MatchingEach US author matched to one doctor in CMS Open Payments by name, state and city. Ambiguous matches are left out.
    3 · PaymentsFees, grants, royalties, travel and meals that companies reported in the full calendar years inside the 36 months before the paper was submitted.
    4 · VerdictRed when a paying company or its corporate group is missing from the declaration. An AI model reads partial declarations.

    A red flag is a question for an editor, not a finding of misconduct. Journal policies differ on what must be declared, and some disclosures reach the editor without being printed. Only US clinicians appear in the payment record. Authors are anonymised to specialty and US region; amounts are rounded to $1,000, and amounts over $100,000 appear only as bands. We checked 43 red cases by hand. The last random check found 14 of 15 correct, and every error we found was traced and fixed in the code.

    Data: Europe PMC (papers), CMS Open Payments 2020–2025 (payments).
